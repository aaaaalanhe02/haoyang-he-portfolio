Metal–halide perovskites are among the most promising materials for next-generation
solar cells and light emitters, but their soft ionic lattice lets ions drift under an
applied bias. That slow ionic motion couples to the fast electronic transport that
actually does the work in a device — and the interference shows up as the notorious
**current–voltage (J–V) hysteresis** that makes these devices hard to characterise and
to trust.

## What I'm probing

My MSc project sits at UCL's clean-energy materials group (supervisor:
Mojtaba Abdi-Jalebi). I'm investigating how **bias-induced ion migration** affects the
internal electric field, charge-extraction kinetics and hysteresis of perovskite
optoelectronic devices — with the goal of separating *ionic redistribution* from
*fast electronic transport* so that measured device behaviour can be interpreted
defensibly.

## Approach

- Analyse **photocurrent amplitude, polarity and relaxation** to disentangle the ionic
  and electronic contributions to the response.
- Develop **ionic–electronic transport models** with Python-based nonlinear fitting to
  extract ionic relaxation times and benchmark devices against one another.
- Perform **thin-film PVD and sample preparation** for the optical and structural
  characterisation workflows that feed the analysis.

## Literature groundwork

Before the experimental work I wrote a review — *Elucidating the Correlation Between
Bias-Induced Ion Migration and Charge Extraction Kinetics in Perovskite Devices* —
mapping the field across J–V scan hysteresis, bias-dependent extraction, time-resolved
probes of ionic–electronic coupling, and the interfacial-engineering strategies used to
tame it. It frames the open questions this project is trying to answer.
