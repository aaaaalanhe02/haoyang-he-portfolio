Spin one cylinder inside another and, past a critical speed, the fluid between them
organises itself into neat stacked vortices — **Taylor–Couette flow**. It's a beautiful,
well-studied instability for simple fluids, and a useful model system for the more
complex rheology of biological fluids like blood.

## What I studied

Using **rotational rheometry**, I characterised the secondary flow patterns that form in
Taylor–Couette cylinders above a **critical Reynolds number**, systematically varying the
viscosity of Newtonian fluids.

## Result

I extended **Ellenberger (1985)** from Newtonian to **non-Newtonian** fluids,
demonstrating a **consistent critical modified Reynolds number across both fluid types** —
evidence that the onset of the instability can be captured by a single scaling once
viscosity is treated correctly.
