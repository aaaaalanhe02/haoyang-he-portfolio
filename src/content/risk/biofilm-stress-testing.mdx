Biofilms are bacterial communities held together by a self-produced matrix that behaves like
a soft material: it resists stress up to a point, then yields. My honours project asked which
components of that matrix hold it together, and how much stress it can take before it fails.

## What I did

- Designed and led a **10-week study** comparing **four gene-deletion mutants with the wild
  type**, using **amplitude-sweep stress tests** to find the strain limit at which each
  material fails.
- **Quantified the impact precisely:** removing the TasA gene cut stiffness (storage
  modulus) by **over 75%** and raised the failure threshold **five-fold**.
- Built a **reproducible Python workflow** — data cleaning, validation, interpolation and
  **Welch's t-tests** — that cut manual analysis time and gave statistically robust
  comparisons across **30+ datasheets**.

## Risk lens

An amplitude sweep is a stress test in the literal sense: raise the load step by step and
record the point at which the system gives way. Stress testing a loan book follows the same
logic, applying progressively severe scenarios to find where losses would breach risk
appetite. The statistics carry over too: validated data, like-for-like comparisons, and
tests that tell a real difference from noise.
