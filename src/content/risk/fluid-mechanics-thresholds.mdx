Spin one cylinder inside another and, past a critical speed, a smooth flow suddenly
reorganises into stacked vortices (Taylor–Couette flow). Nothing changes gradually: the
system crosses a threshold and switches regime.

## What I did

- Identified the **critical threshold (Reynolds number)** at which the flow shifts regime,
  by systematically varying fluid viscosity in **rotational rheometry**.
- Extended a published **1985 model** (Ellenberger) to **non-Newtonian fluids**, and built a
  **Python nonlinear regression model** that narrowed the parameter range describing the
  transition.

## Risk lens

Many risks behave like this flow: stable until a threshold, then a sudden change of regime.
Finding that threshold from data, and knowing how precisely it is pinned down, is the
thinking behind early-warning indicators and risk-appetite limits.
