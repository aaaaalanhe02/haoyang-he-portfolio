My MSc research project, in the Functional Materials & Energy Devices (FMED) group at UCL,
studied perovskite optoelectronic devices. Their performance drifts over time because ions
migrate slowly under an applied bias while electrons respond almost instantly, so the raw
measurements mix two very different effects together.

## What I did

- Built **quantitative models in Python (nonlinear regression)** to fit **time-dependent
  relaxation data**, extract the key parameters and **benchmark performance across
  devices**.
- **Separated slow ionic drift from fast electronic effects** in photocurrent data,
  attributing each change in performance to its **root-cause driver rather than to noise**.

## Risk lens

This is model calibration and performance attribution. Fit a model to noisy,
time-dependent data; check that its parameters are stable and meaningful; then explain
*why* performance moved. In a credit portfolio the drivers are different — portfolio mix,
macroeconomic conditions, changes in underwriting — but the discipline of separating a slow
structural trend from short-term noise is the same.
